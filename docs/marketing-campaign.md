# Evo Stays launch campaign

Status: draft, 2026-10-23. The product is pre-launch ("Register interest" is the only call to action), so the whole campaign is built to fill a waitlist, convert it into demos, and turn the first 5-10 customers into proof.

## 1. Positioning

**Who it's for:** short-let management companies and serviced-accommodation operators that run their *own* cleaning team (employed or regular contractors) across a portfolio of client properties. Not solo Airbnb hosts, and not people who want to hire strangers from a marketplace.

**The gap we sit in.** The research I could find (vendor sites only; no independent market data turned up) shows the incumbents lean two ways: marketplaces that find you a cleaner (Turno, Hospitable's cleaner marketplace) and broad operations platforms (Breezeway, Properly). Our product is the operations hub for a company that already has its cleaners and wants them to run smoothly. Say that plainly.

**One-line pitch:** *Your cleaners, your clients, your portfolio, in one place instead of a spreadsheet and a group chat.*

**Proof points to lead with (all real, from the app):**
1. Auto-assigns by familiarity (cleaner who already knows the property), not just whoever is free.
2. Same-day turnovers jump the queue with a finish-by time worked out for you.
3. Guided checklist with before/after photos; steps can't be faked from the browser.
4. Cleaners report facts, the system decides urgency (no cleaner has to judge severity).
5. Invoices generated from real check-in/check-out times, per client.
6. Linen company gets its own login with a van schedule, and is told when a clean moves or cancels. **Nobody else I found does this. It's the headline differentiator.**
7. Nine PMS integrations (Hostify, Guesty, Hostaway, Lodgify, OwnerRez, Hospitable, Smoobu, Hostfully, Beds24) plus iCal.
8. Installable phone app with push alerts.

## 2. What to produce (in priority order)

### A. Foundations (weeks 1-2): without these nothing else works
| Asset | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Landing page upgrade** | The current page is solid but the only CTA is a form with no reason to act. | Add a 60-90s product video above the fold, a "founding customer" offer, and a one-question reason to sign up. Add a sample-data **live demo login** so people try it without a call. |
| **Demo video (90s)** | The single most reusable asset: site, ads, DMs, email. | Screen-record a real day: booking arrives, clean auto-assigned, cleaner checks in on phone, photo, issue flagged, invoice made. No voiceover jargon. Captions on. |
| **Same-day-turnover vertical clip (20-30s)** | Social-native. | One scenario: "guest checks out 11:00, next arrives 14:00": show the schedule flag, the finish-by time, the push alert on a phone. Cut 3 versions as Reels/Shorts/LinkedIn. |
| **Founding customer offer** | Gives a reason to commit now and gives us case studies. | e.g. first 10 companies: free onboarding + discounted rate locked for 12 months, in exchange for a case study and a reference call. Decide numbers before launch. |
| **Tracking** | Know which channel works. | UTM links per channel; the register-interest form should record "where did you hear about us". (Not built yet; small change to `InterestRegistration`.) |

### B. Content that makes people feel the problem (ongoing)
- **"The turnover cost calculator"**: interactive page where an operator enters cleans/week, no-show rate, hours spent in group chats, and sees the monthly cost. Ends with the register CTA. Highest-value lead magnet because it's shareable.
- **Free templates** (PDF/Google Sheet, gated by email): short-let turnover checklist by room, linen par-level sheet, cleaner onboarding pack, "what to do when a cleaner no-shows" playbook. These rank for search and are exactly what our product replaces.
- **3-4 pillar articles**: "How to schedule same-day turnovers without a spreadsheet", "Linen and laundry for short lets: tracking it properly", "Paying cleaners by real hours: how to invoice clients fairly", "Managing a cleaning team vs using a marketplace". Honest comparison pages ("Evo Stays vs Turno", "vs Breezeway") should say who each is for; they convert well because people search for them.
- **Founder-led LinkedIn posts, 3/week**: operator war stories, a screenshot per post, one lesson. Founder posts outperform company pages at this stage.

### C. Outbound (weeks 2-6): the fastest route to the first customers
Short-let operators are a reachable, finite list. Build a list of 200-300 UK management companies (Companies House SIC codes 55200/68320, STAA member list, Airbnb/Booking.com "managed by" listings, PMS partner directories, LinkedIn search for "serviced accommodation", "short let", "property manager").
- Personalised email + LinkedIn message referencing their portfolio size, with the 90s video, not a pitch deck. Offer the founding-customer slot.
- Target 20 new conversations a week; expect single-digit-percent replies, so measure it before scaling.
- Follow up by phone for the best 50: this is a relationship-sized market.

### D. Community and partnerships
- **PMS marketplaces and partners.** Already integrated with nine PMSs, so apply to each one's partner/integration directory and ask for a listing. Free, targeted, and credible.
- **Linen and laundry companies as a channel.** Each customer's linen supplier gets a login and sees the product daily. Offer them a referral arrangement: they recommend us to their other short-let clients. This is a built-in viral loop none of the competitors has.
- **Trade body:** STAA (UK Short Term Accommodation Association) and ASAP. Seek a member-newsletter feature, an event stand, or a webinar slot.
- **Communities:** short-let and serviced-accommodation groups on Facebook, LinkedIn and Reddit. I couldn't verify which UK groups are active or their rules, so list them manually. Rule: answer questions with real help, disclose you built the product, never drop links cold.
- **Podcasts:** pitch as a guest to short-term-rental operator podcasts with the story of why you built it (operator pain, not product features).

### E. Launch moment (around go-live)
- **Product Hunt / Hacker News "Show HN"**: low fit for this audience (operators, not tech people) but cheap, and gives backlinks and a credibility badge. Don't make it the centrepiece.
- **Webinar: "Run a 50-property turnover operation without a spreadsheet"**, a live demo with one founding customer. Record it and cut into clips.
- **Case study #1** the moment a customer has 30 days of data: before/after numbers (time spent coordinating, missed cleans, invoicing time).

### F. Paid (only after outbound proves the message)
- LinkedIn ads at job titles (operations manager, property manager, founder at short-let companies) with the demo video; retargeting website visitors with the calculator.
- Google search on "short let cleaning management software", "turnover scheduling software", and competitor names. Small budget first (about £15-30/day) to test.

## 3. Sequencing

| When | Do |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1-2 | Landing page + demo login, 90s video, source tracking, founding offer, build target list |
| Weeks 2-4 | Start outbound, publish calculator + 2 templates, daily LinkedIn presence, apply to PMS directories |
| Weeks 4-8 | Run demos, onboard first 3-5 founding customers, approach linen companies and STAA |
| Weeks 8-12 | Case study, comparison pages, webinar, test small paid budget |
| After | Double down on whichever channel produced the most demos per hour spent |

## 4. Goals (suggested, adjust to your appetite)
- 12 weeks: 300 targeted contacts reached, 150 waitlist sign-ups, 40 demos, 5-10 founding customers.
- Leading indicators to watch weekly: reply rate, demo-booked rate, demo-to-customer rate, sign-up source.

## 5. Things to settle before launching
1. **Pricing.** I couldn't find reliable competitor per-property pricing (listing sites conflict). Check Turno, Breezeway and Properly pricing pages directly, then decide ours.
2. **Brand voice and logo usage**, and which market first (UK only? the app talks "short-let", so I assumed UK).
3. **Who does outreach and demos**: this plan assumes the founder at the start.
4. **Privacy/consent**: cold email and the waitlist need GDPR/PECR-compliant wording and an unsubscribe route.
5. **Real phone push test** is still outstanding (see memory note); do it before showing push alerts in any video or claiming it in copy.

## 6. What I'd build in the product to support this
- Live demo login with seeded data (the seed script already exists).
- "Where did you hear about us" + UTM capture on the register-interest form.
- A public, shareable turnover cost calculator page.
- Linen company invite/referral link.
