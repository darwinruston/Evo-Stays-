## Text-to-speech prompt

Paste this into the voice tool's style or direction field, then the lines for each shot one at a time so the timing can be adjusted per scene.

> A warm, confident British female or male voice (southern English accent, not posh, not regional), like a friendly operations manager explaining a tool to a colleague. Calm, clear and unhurried, about 150 words a minute, with a slight smile in the voice. No sales shouting, no "announcer" tone, no dramatic pauses. Short natural breaths between sentences. Stress the useful words ("already knows", "finish-by", "real times"). Say "Evo Stays" as two clear words with the same stress on each. Read "finish-by" as "finish by". End the final line on a gentle downward tone.

Notes for choosing a voice:
- Generate three or four candidates for shot 3 (the longest line) and pick on how it sounds there.
- Export each shot's line as its own audio file (shot-01.mp3 and so on), so a scene can be re-recorded without redoing the lot.
- If a tool mispronounces "Evo", respell it in the prompt (for example "Eevo" or "Evvo") and keep the spelling in the captions as "Evo".
- Check the licence of whichever voice you use covers commercial advertising.

## Captions and music
- Burn in captions (white text, dark soft shadow) for every line. Most people watch on mute.
- Light, upbeat instrumental, quiet under the voice and lifted slightly during the title and end cards. Use royalty-free music with a licence that allows advertising.

## Before recording
- Seed the demo company so every screen has data: today's cleans, one same-day turnover, one double-booked cleaner, an open issue, low stock on one item, one linen visit, and a week of invoice-ready cleans.
- Hide anything personal: use only demo names (Harbour Lets, Peak Retreats, Riverside Loft and so on).
- Record at 1920 by 1080 for the desktop scenes and a phone-sized window (390 by 844) for the cleaner scenes.
