# Trial-stage campaign: build interest while a few companies try Evo Stays

Draft, 26/11/2026. This covers the stage we are in now. The wider launch plan is in
[../marketing-campaign.md](../marketing-campaign.md) and still applies afterwards.

## The idea in one line

**Make a few companies succeed, record honestly what changed for them, and tell that story as it happens.**
Right now proof is the thing we do not have. Interest built without it is hard to keep.

## Who we are talking to

Short-let management and cleaning companies that run their **own** cleaners, roughly 5 to 60 properties.
Not people looking to hire a cleaner from a marketplace.

**What we say:** *Your channel manager looks after the bookings. Evo Stays looks after the property:
the codes, the cleans, the safety dates and the linen, in one place for the whole team.*

Lead with the three things hardest to find elsewhere (as far as our research went, not a proven claim):
safety dates with reminders and certificates, a login for the linen company, and cleaner hours that scale
with team size and go to Xero.

## Phase 1: the trial (now to end of January)

**Timing.** December is busy for short lets. Start with our own first company (JFMS) now, and bring the
others on from the second week of January.

**How many:** three to five. More than that and we cannot support them properly.

**Pick companies that** already have their own cleaners, feel a real pain (missed cleans, messages
everywhere, safety dates in a spreadsheet), will talk to us weekly, and ideally use Hostify.

**The deal (suggested):** free for 8 weeks and we set it up for them. In return, a 20-minute call each
week, honest feedback, and permission to use their results if they are happy with them. Decide before
offering it what happens afterwards, including whether early companies get a lower rate.

### Measure before they start

This is the step that cannot be done later. Ask each company, on day one:

1. How many cleans a week?
2. How long do you spend each week arranging them and chasing?
3. How many cleans were missed, late or doubled up last month?
4. How long does it take to work out what to pay cleaners?
5. Where are your safety certificate dates kept, and has one ever lapsed?

Ask the same again at week 4 and week 8. The difference is the case study.

### Weekly call, five questions

1. What did you use this week, and what did you do outside it instead?
2. What annoyed your cleaners?
3. What nearly went wrong?
4. What would you miss if we switched it off tomorrow?
5. Who else should see this?

## Phase 2: build interest alongside (December to March)

Aim for about **four hours a week**, every week. Steady beats a burst.

| Each week | Time |
|---|---|
| Two founder posts on LinkedIn, from a personal profile | 1 hour |
| Reply helpfully in two or three short-let groups, saying you build the product | 1 hour |
| Contact five companies personally | 1.5 hours |
| Note who replied and why | 30 minutes |

**Something worth giving away.** A one-page *UK short-let safety dates checklist*: what to check, how
often, and what to keep. It suits our strength and people save it. Have the contents checked, label it a
starting list rather than legal advice, and ask for an email address to download it.

**Short clips (20 to 30 seconds, screen recordings of the demo company):**
a repeating clean being set up; a cleaner's phone from arrival to check-out; a safety date turning into a
task; the linen company seeing a clean move.

**Warm routes first.** Trial companies' contacts, their linen companies (who see the product every day),
accountants who work with short lets and use Xero, and Hostify's own user community.

## Phase 3: turn proof into sign-ups (February to April)

2. Put it on the website and lead outreach with it.

## Ready-to-use copy

Fill the square brackets. Never fill one with a guess.

### Inviting a company to the trial

> Hi [name], I'm building Evo Stays, which keeps cleans, access codes, safety dates and linen in one
> place for short-let teams with their own cleaners. We're taking on a few companies to try it free for
> 8 weeks, set up by us, in return for a short weekly chat. [Something specific about their business]
> made me think of you. Could I show you for 20 minutes? If not, no problem at all, just say and I won't
> follow up.

### One follow-up, a week later

> Hi [name], just once more in case it got buried. Happy to send a 90-second video instead of a call.
> If it's not for you, ignore this and I'll leave it there.

### LinkedIn posts

**1. Why**
> Our cleans lived in a group chat, the key safe codes in someone's notes and the gas safety dates in a
> spreadsheet nobody opened. So we started building one place for all of it. We're taking on a few
> companies to try it. If you run your own cleaners, I'd like to hear what breaks for you first.

**2. A small, real problem**
> A cleaner in the kitchen, then the store cupboard, then back to the kitchen to count stock. Small
> thing, every single clean. We now walk them through it room by room. What's your version of this?

**3. Building in the open**
> This week our team told us [real feedback]. We changed [what we changed]. [Screenshot]

**4. After the trial**
> After 8 weeks, [company] went from [before] to [after]. Their words: "[approved quote]".

## Contacting people within the rules

- Under the PECR marketing rules, emails to **limited companies and LLPs** do not need prior consent, but
  must say who we are and offer a way to opt out, which we must honour.
- **Sole traders and some partnerships** are treated like individuals: generally consent first.
- If unsure which they are, treat them as individuals.
- Data protection law still applies to named contacts: keep a record of where each came from and of every
  opt-out.

Source: [ICO, business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/).
Check it before sending, as the rules have been changing.

## What counts as working

These are targets to aim at, not industry benchmarks. We have none.

| By | Aim |
|---|---|
| End of December | Own company using it for every clean |
| End of January | 3 outside companies set up, starting figures recorded |
| End of March | 2 of them still using it weekly and willing to be quoted; 50 on the interest list |
| End of April | 1 published case study; 10 demos held; first paying company |

**Stop and fix, rather than market harder, if** a trial company stops using it, or cleaners avoid it.
